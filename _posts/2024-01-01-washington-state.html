Walking around the University of Washington campus in January was one of those days that felt exactly right to me: cold, quiet, hardly anyone else around. I've never understood people who complain about the cold; it's the weather that makes me want to keep walking instead of finding somewhere to sit down.

Cape Flattery was the real reason for the trip, though, and it was out of this world. The trees right at the edge grow bent and curved, worn into that shape by years of wind off the strait, and the coastline out there is more dramatic than anything a map suggests. Ruby Beach a few days later had the same feeling with arches and cliffs cut into the coast, sea stacks that look too massive to actually be standing there, driftwood scattered around like the ocean had just tossed it up. Both places were pristine in a way that's hard to explain until you're standing in front of it.
